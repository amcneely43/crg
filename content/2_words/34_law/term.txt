Etymology: From Old English <em>lagu</em> (that which is laid down or fixed). In Kant, law is the form through which community becomes universal — the nomos that binds without particularizing. For Esposito, law is the immunitary apparatus: the structure that protects community by separating individuals from one another. Distinct from Rights (entitlement) and from Tradition (custom) — Law is the structure itself, the ordering that simultaneously constitutes and limits the common.
