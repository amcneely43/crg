Note: <p><em>isolated (adj.)</em></p><p>"standing detached from others of its kind," 1740, a rendering into English of French <em>isolé</em> "isolated" (17c.), from Italian <em>isolato</em>, from Latin <em>insulatus</em> "made into an island," from <em>insula</em> "island" (see isle (n.)). English at first used the French word (<em>isole</em>, also <em>isole'd</em>, c. 1750), then after isolate (v.) became an English word, isolated became its past participle.</p>

Blurb: <p>The Community Reading Group is a gathering committed to the study of community as a political category in popular, philosophical, and legal discourse. We focus on literature that complicates affirmative invocations of community in both grassroots and organizational contexts as the proper aim of activism. Examples: community building, resilience, preservation, displacement, health, rights, and wealth.</p>
