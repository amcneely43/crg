Note: <p><strong><em>beast (n.)</em></strong></p><p>c. 1200, <em>beste</em>, "one of the lower animals" (opposed to man), especially "a four-footed animal," also "a marvelous creature, a monster" (mermaids, werewolves, lamia, satyrs, the beast of the Apocalypse), "a brutish or stupid man," from Old French beste "animal, wild beast," figuratively "fool, idiot" (11c., Modern French bête), from Vulgar Latin <em>*besta</em>, from Latin <em>bestia</em> "beast, wild animal," which is of unknown origin.</p><p>The word was used in Middle English to translate Latin animal. It displaced Old English <em>deor</em> (see deer) as the generic word for "wild creature," only to be ousted 16c. by animal.</p><p></p>
