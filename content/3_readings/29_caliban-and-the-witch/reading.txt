Note: <p><strong><em>care (n.)</em></strong></p><p>Old English <em>caru</em>, <em>cearu</em> "sorrow, anxiety, grief," also "burdens of mind; serious mental attention," in late Old English also "concern, anxiety caused by apprehension of evil or the weight of many burdens," from Proto-Germanic <em>*karō</em> "lament; grief, care" (source also of Old Saxon <em>kara</em> "sorrow;" Old High German chara "wail, lament;" Gothic kara "sorrow, trouble, care."</p>

----

Blurb: <p>I wanted to share with you and the group that my interest in these readings is based on my own dive into disability justice this year. I am immunocompromised and identify as disabled, although “invisibly.” This material / biological difference being heightened during the pandemic, I’ve been interested in readings that offer insight on our collective relationship with illness - the social control of our bodies and split between body &amp; mind - and the necessity / possibility of community care through mutual aid (Care Work).</p>
